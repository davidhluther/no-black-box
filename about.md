# About David Luther

This page is for anyone deciding whether to hire or work with me. It covers how I think about technology work, and the repo around it is the evidence.

## The point of view

Most of the technology a small organization depends on sits in a black box. Some of that is honest complexity. Some of it is a business model, because knowledge that stays hidden can be sold back by the hour. I think that's backwards for the people who need it most. A hair salon or a small food bank shouldn't pay someone to explain how its own tools work, and when it can't, it serves fewer people.

So my default is to explain the thing in plain words, give away the complete method, and charge only for what is truly scarce, which is my time fitting the method to one organization. I never hold back a step to create a sale. If a guide can't be followed by a volunteer on a Tuesday evening, I count that as my failure.

## How I work

- **Fit over template.** Software and advice sold identically to everyone fits nobody well. Every kit here says how to adapt it, and where the reader has real choices.
- **Show the evidence.** A claim gets a source. A number in a report traces back to the document it came from. When I can't verify something, I say "not verified" and say what I checked.
- **Read-only first.** Tools that touch someone's books, data, or website start by looking and never changing. The treasurer kit is built around that promise.
- **Failures get recorded.** A job that quietly stops running is worse than one that fails loudly. I build checks, retries, and a plain note to a person.
- **Plain language is a design constraint.** Every technical term is defined where it first appears, each setup step says how you'll know it worked, and each known problem has a code and a fix.
- **Test with a newcomer.** A guide is finished when someone with no background can follow it, not when I can.

## What this repo shows
