# Home and Office Network Basics

Your network is the set of devices that share one internet connection, held together by a router (the box that connects your devices to the internet, like a switchboard operator routing calls). This guide covers the five settings that matter for a small organization, in plain steps. You don't need to know anything about networking first.

Terms are in the [glossary](glossary.md). Every router's screen looks a little different, so these steps tell you what to look for, not where to click.

1. **NB-FR-05-1.** Find your router and write down its make, model, and the label on the bottom. The label often lists a network name, a Wi-Fi password, and an address for the admin page.
   You'll know it worked when you have a note with those items. If the label is missing, search the model on its maker's support site (E11 in the [shared fixes](getting-help.md#shared-fixes)).
2. **NB-FR-05-2.** Open the router's admin page (the settings screen) by typing the address from the label into your browser's address bar, then sign in.
   You'll know it worked when you see a settings screen. If the sign-in on the label doesn't work, the router may have been changed before. A reset button, usually a pinhole, returns it to the label's settings, but it erases the setup, so ask whoever installed it first.
3. **NB-FR-05-3.** Change the admin sign-in from the factory default to a long unique one, and store it in your password manager (an app that remembers passwords for you).
   You'll know it worked when the old sign-in is refused.
4. **NB-FR-05-4.** Check the Wi-Fi security setting. Choose the newest option offered, such as WPA3 (the current lock for Wi-Fi), or WPA2 if that's the best your router has. Never choose "Open" or "WEP."
   You'll know it worked when the router lists a WPA setting.
5. **NB-FR-05-5.** Make a guest network (a second Wi-Fi name for visitors) and tell clients and volunteers to use it. Keep staff and work devices on the main network.
   You'll know it worked when your phone sees two names and the guest one can't open your office printer.
6. **NB-FR-05-6.** Turn on automatic updates for the router's software (called firmware). If there is no automatic option, put a reminder on your calendar every three months to check.
   You'll know it worked when the page shows a recent update date.
7. **NB-FR-05-7.** Write one page for the organization: the router's make and model, where the admin sign-in is stored, who has it, and who to call. Keep it with your other records.
   You'll know it worked when a second person can find it without asking you.

## When something stops working

Restart in this order. Unplug the router, wait 30 seconds, then plug it back in and wait two minutes. If one device is the problem, forget the Wi-Fi network on that device and join again. If everything is down, call your internet provider and tell them which lights on the router are on or off.

> **How to get help.** Write down the [step ID](glossary.md#step-id) where you got stuck (it looks like NB-FR-05-3). If your [kit](glossary.md#kit) has a [check-up](glossary.md#check-up) button or command, run it and copy the [support code](glossary.md#support-code) it prints.
