# What David Built

This page is for recruiters, clients and curious readers. It tells the story of the kit in plain English.

## The problem

Small teams get asked to run ads and social accounts with no training. The advice online contradicts itself, platform rules change every few months, and the [AI models](glossary.md#ai-model) that write copy are happy to invent claims, overshoot [character limits](glossary.md#character-limit) and produce posts that all sound alike. The people who understand this charge for it, and the explanation stays in their heads.

## What David made

David Luther, an SEO and content strategist who runs Pigasus Group, built this kit by combining three working methods he already used in his own content operation.

From his image workflow he took the picture path. It tries [Canva](glossary.md#canva) first, then an [image service](glossary.md#image-service) only if the user has supplied their own key, and always produces a written [designer brief](glossary.md#designer-brief) as the fallback. From his LinkedIn work he took the idea of adapting one point per audience instead of pasting the same text everywhere. From a small caption tool he built for his own consultancy's blog, he took the rule that a post should land one idea fully rather than summarize an article.

The new parts are the ones nobody had written down for a beginner. The kit separates facts it verified on a platform's own help page from guidance it can only link to. It makes every claim in an ad pass through a short checklist before anyone can copy it. It treats test [variants](glossary.md#variant) as a record to learn from, with the limits of small samples spelled out. It also ships a check-up button inside a Google Sheet that produces a short support code, so a person who gets stuck can say exactly where.

## What it changed

The intended change is confidence, and a cold-reader review with a real beginner is still pending. A reader should know which numbers are rules and which are habits, know where to look when a rule changes, and have a written voice that stops every post sounding like a template. Ad drafts come with the evidence trail attached, which makes review quicker for a board or a boss.

## Skills it shows

- Turning an expert's tacit knowledge into plain, step-numbered instructions for beginners.
- Honest handling of facts, with a visible line between verified rules and linked guidance.
- Prompt and [skill](glossary.md#skill) design that works across several AI products, not just one.
- Marketing judgment about testing, claims and platform fit.
- Safe tooling, such as a script that only reads keys from the user's own settings and never prints them.
- Support design, with step IDs, problem codes and a no-secrets check-up report.
- Accessible content, including [alt text](glossary.md#alt-text) for every picture.

## Why it exists

David's view is that explaining a thing plainly is the fairest way to share it. The knowledge here is free and complete. Paid help from Pigasus Group buys his time to fit it to one organization, never a withheld step.
