# Ad and Social: Start Here

You get ad copy, ad pictures and social posts for X, Facebook and Instagram that sound like your organization, written with an [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model) and checked against rules you can read. Along the way you get a plain record of what you tried, so you learn which wording works for your own audience.

Here is the black box this kit opens. Advertising looks like a trade secret. Agencies and ad tools sell the same templates to everyone, quote you a fee to answer simple questions ("How long can a [headline](glossary.md#headline) be?", "What size is an Instagram picture?"), and keep the reasoning to themselves. A hair salon or a small food bank should not have to pay someone to learn that. Everything here is written out, including the parts that are only guidance and the places where the rules change often.

## Who it's for

Picture a salon owner who wants a Facebook ad for new-client week, a food bank coordinator who needs a Saturday volunteer post for three platforms, or a volunteer who runs the social accounts after work. No design or marketing background needed, and no command line for the free and low-cost versions.

## What you will have at the end

- A one-page "[voice sheet](glossary.md#voice-sheet)" that tells any AI model how your organization sounds, what it offers, and what it must never claim.
- Search ad text (headlines and descriptions) and social ad text, with several versions to test against each other.
- Posts for X, Facebook and Instagram, each rewritten for its platform from one idea, with captions and alt text (a short written description of a picture, read aloud to people who can't see it).
- Pictures for those ads and posts, made in Canva (a free online design tool), or from an [image service](glossary.md#image-service) if you choose that route, or a ready-to-send brief for a designer.
- A [claims checklist](glossary.md#claims-checklist), so nothing you publish promises more than you can prove.

## How it works

1. You fill in the voice sheet once.
2. You tell the AI model the idea, the offer and where it will run.
3. It drafts, then lists every claim it made so you can confirm each one.
4. You check the character counts in the platform's own tool, because platform limits change.
5. You make or brief the picture, and post.

The kit never posts or spends money for you. You publish through the platform's own screens.
