# Start Here for Owner's Numbers

Once a month you will get a one-page scorecard of your own business, built from files you already have. It tells you in two minutes whether the month paid for itself, what actually earns money, who your customers were, how full your calendar was, and the one thing to look at next. You also get a library of 13 plain-language money measures (what it costs to serve a customer, where you break even, how long your cash would last) and templates for a quarterly report, a short advisory letter and a 12-month roadmap.

It is for the owner of a hair salon, a repair shop, a clinic, a small restaurant, or any business that sells services or goods and has three [exports](glossary.md#export) sitting in different programs. It also suits a volunteer treasurer or a board member who wants to read the numbers without asking someone to translate.

## What you will have at the end

- A monthly Owner's Scorecard on one page, from your point-of-sale, bank and booking exports.
- A pricing [what-if](glossary.md#what-if) that shows how much business a price change can lose before it stops paying.
- Templates for a quarterly report, an advisory letter and a 12-month roadmap.
- A [check-up](glossary.md#check-up) button that tells you if the setup is right.
- A rule that protects you from fooling yourself. Any number built on a guess is labeled a [what-if](glossary.md#what-if), never presented as a [finding](glossary.md#finding).

## The black box, once

So much of how business numbers work stays locked up. A bookkeeper or a consultant applies the same template to every customer, a software subscription sells everyone the same dashboard, and the question you really have ("can I afford to hire?") waits for a meeting. There is no reason a hair salon or a small food bank should pay someone to answer a simple question about its own money. The math here is ordinary arithmetic, it is all written out, and nothing is held back.

## How it works

You already have the raw material. Your [point-of-sale system](glossary.md#point-of-sale-system) can [export](glossary.md#export) your sales, your bank lets you download a [CSV](glossary.md#csv), and your booking tool can print a report. You put those in a [Google Sheets](glossary.md#google-sheets) file, or give them to an [AI Project](glossary.md#ai-project), or run a small [Python](glossary.md#python) program. All three build the same page.

A scorecard section only calls something a finding when every input is yours. If you have not told it your [fixed costs](glossary.md#fixed-cost), for instance, it borrows an example and labels the result a what-if. That keeps the page honest on the first day, when your data is thin, and gets more reliable as you add numbers.

## What this does not do

It does not give tax, legal or investment advice, does not file anything, and does not connect to your bank. The scorecard only reads the files you hand it, and what to decide stays with you, because it shows what your own numbers say and stops.
