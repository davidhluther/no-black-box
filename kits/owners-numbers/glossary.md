# Glossary

Plain meanings for every technical term in this kit. Entries are in alphabetical order.

**AI model.** A computer program trained on huge amounts of writing that answers questions and drafts text. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are products built on AI models.

**AI Project.** A saved workspace inside ChatGPT or Claude where you keep standing instructions and reference files, so you do not have to repeat them in every chat. Think of it as a desk that stays set up between visits.

**Apps Script.** Google's built-in way to add small programs to a Google Sheet. It is how the check-up button works. It runs inside your own Google account.

**Break-even.** The amount you have to sell in a month before you stop losing money. Below it you lose money, above it you keep some.

**Capacity use.** How much of the time or seats you could sell you actually sold, as a share. If you could book 100 hours and booked 66, capacity use is 66%.

**Cash conversion.** The number of days between paying for the things you sell and getting paid for them. A big number means your money sits trapped for a while.

**Check-up.** A button in the Sheet (Help menu, then Run check-up) that tests whether your setup is right and prints a short support code. It looks at tab names and counts, never at what is in your cells.

**Command line.** A window where you type short instructions to your computer instead of clicking. The full version uses it for a few copy-and-paste commands. Also called the terminal.

**Contribution margin.** What one sale keeps after the direct cost of making that sale. If a haircut sells for $55 and the supplies cost $6, it keeps $49.

**Cost to serve.** What a service, product line or customer really costs once a fair share of shared costs like rent is added to its direct costs.

**CSV.** A plain text file where each row of a spreadsheet is one line and the columns are separated by commas. Almost every program can export one, and every spreadsheet can open one.

**Custom GPT.** A version of ChatGPT you set up with your own instructions and files. It is one of the ways to use this kit's instructions.

**Customer lifetime value.** What a customer is worth to you over all the years they keep coming, not just one visit. The kit uses yearly margin and how often customers return.

**Export.** A file you download from another program (your register, your bank, your booking tool) so you can use its data somewhere else.

**Finding.** A statement built only on your own real numbers. Compare with a what-if.

**Fixed cost.** A cost that arrives every month whether you sell a lot or a little, such as rent.

**Formula.** A short instruction typed into a spreadsheet cell, starting with an equals sign, that calculates a result from other cells.

**Google Sheets.** Google's free spreadsheet app. You open it in a web browser at sheets.google.com.

**Headcount payback.** How many months it takes for a new hire to earn back what they cost you, including the one-time cost of hiring and training them.

**Knowledge file.** A document you upload to a Custom GPT or an AI Project so the AI model can read it every time.

**Margin.** The share of the price you keep as profit. A $100 sale with $40 of profit has a 40% margin. Not the same as markup.

**Markup.** The profit as a share of what the thing cost you, not of the price. The same $100 sale that cost $60 has a markup of about 67% and a margin of 40%.

**Metric.** A single number that answers a question about your business, such as "how much do I need to sell to break even?"

**Overhead.** Costs that keep the doors open but do not belong to one sale, like rent, insurance and software.

**Point-of-sale system.** The register or app where you ring up sales, such as Square, Toast or Shopify. It is the source of your sales export.

**Python.** A programming language. The full version uses small Python programs to build the scorecard. You do not need to write any.

**Retention.** The share of customers who come back year after year. If seven out of ten return, retention is 70%.

**Runway.** How many months your cash would last at the current rate of loss. It only makes sense in a month where you spent more than you took in.

**Script.** A small program saved as a file. The full version's scorecard is built by scripts that only read your files.

**Support code.** A short code the check-up prints, such as ON-FR-03-1-E03-v0.1.0-7KQ. It tells whoever is helping which step, which problem and which kit version.

**Tab.** One of the labeled pages along the bottom of a spreadsheet. A single Google Sheets file can hold many.

**Variable cost.** A cost that goes up and down with how much you sell, such as supplies, card fees and commissions.

**What-if.** A result worked out with at least one number you assumed instead of measured. It shows how the math works and what could happen. It is never a finding about your business.

**Working capital.** What you could pay with in the short run (cash, money owed to you, stock) minus what you must pay in the short run (bills and loan payments due within a year).
