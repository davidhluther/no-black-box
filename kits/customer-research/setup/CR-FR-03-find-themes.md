# CR-FR-03: Find Themes

You will group your quotes into [themes](../glossary.md#theme) and turn them into a short list of customer words. Time: about an hour. A [theme](../glossary.md#theme) is a group of quotes about the same thing. Your [prompts](../glossary.md#prompt) P2, P3, and P4 in [prompts.md](../free/prompts.md) do the sorting. You judge the result.

1. **CR-FR-03-1.** In the Quotes [tab](../glossary.md#tab), select the quote_id and [verbatim](../glossary.md#verbatim) columns for the first 30 to 60 rows, copy them, and paste prompt P2 into a free [AI model](../glossary.md#ai-model) with the quotes at the end. Send it.
   You'll know it worked when the AI model returns four to eight named themes with quote IDs and sample phrases. If it cuts off, send fewer quotes, which is E15. If the themes use business language instead of your customers' words, ask it to rename each theme using the customers' own phrases.

2. **CR-FR-03-2.** Copy each theme into one row of the Themes tab: the theme name, the plain description, the quote count, the example quote IDs, and the customer words.
   You'll know it worked when each row has a name and at least one quote ID. If the [check-up](../glossary.md#check-up) lists the Themes tab, it's E04.

3. **CR-FR-03-3.** Write each quote's theme name into the theme column of the Quotes tab (this tagging step is called [coding](../glossary.md#coding)), and the [sentiment](../glossary.md#sentiment) (positive, negative, mixed, or neutral). Do about ten by hand to check the AI model sorted them well, then let it do the rest if the sample was right.
   You'll know it worked when at least nine of your ten spot-checks match your own judgment. If fewer than that, tell the AI model which ones were wrong and why, and ask it to redo the sorting.

4. **CR-FR-03-4.** Paste what you currently say on your sign, website, or flyer into prompt P3, with your themes, and send it. Copy the answer into the our_current_words, gap, and action columns of the Themes tab.
   You'll know it worked when each theme shows whether your words match what customers say. If the AI model starts writing new advertising copy instead, tell it to stay within the table.

5. **CR-FR-03-5.** Make your short list of customer words. Pick the 10 to 15 phrases that appear in several quotes from at least two different sources. Put them in a new block at the bottom of the Themes tab headed "Customer words". Beside each, write the quote IDs that prove it.
   You'll know it worked when every phrase on the list traces to quote IDs. Leave out any phrase you cannot trace.

6. **CR-FR-03-6.** Build one [persona](../glossary.md#persona) card for each kind of customer who shows up in the quotes, usually two to four. Use prompt P4 with only the quotes that fit that kind of customer. Save each card in a Google Doc titled "Persona cards", with the quote IDs under each worry.
   You'll know it worked when each worry on a card points to a quote ID and the card says "not in the evidence" wherever the quotes were silent. A card that fills every gap with confident detail is inventing, so ask the model to cut anything without a quote behind it.

7. **CR-FR-03-7.** Do a stop check. Collect ten more quotes, run them through P2, and see whether they fit your existing themes. If a new theme appears, add it and repeat with another ten.
   You'll know it worked when ten fresh quotes bring no new theme. That is the sign you have enough for a small organization. Note it in the Themes tab. If your total is under 30 quotes, treat every theme as a guess; this is the [sample size](../glossary.md#sample-size) problem.

When you have your themes and customer words, go on to [CR-FR-04](CR-FR-04-test-your-wording.md).
