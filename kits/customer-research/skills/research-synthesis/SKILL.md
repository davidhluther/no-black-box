# Research Synthesis

Works in: Claude Code, Claude.ai, ChatGPT

The collecting skills each produce a list. This skill puts the lists together, so you can see which words and worries appear across sources and which appear in only one. Appearing in several places is the closest thing the research has to proof.

## Rules that never bend

1. Do not collect again. Work only from what the user provides: the Quotes, Sources, and Themes tabs and any reports. If a source is old (older than 30 days for forums and suggestions, two years for reviews), say so and ask whether to refresh it.
2. Every statement traces to quote IDs. If a finding has no quote behind it, delete it.
3. Count everything. Fewer than five quotes is too thin, and a theme backed by one source may be one loud person.
4. Quotes stay word for word.
5. Report gaps as findings. A blank cell in the matrix is a finding.

## Method

1. **Inventory sources.** List each source, how many quotes it gave, and when it was collected. Flag stale or lopsided sources (one source over half).
2. **Build the phrase table.** One row per unique phrase with: phrase, sources it appears in (and a "multi-source" flag if two or more), channel (review, forum, search suggestion, conversation), who says it (customer role), stage of deciding (just noticed a problem, comparing options, ready to act, using it and reflecting), theme, and the quote IDs.
3. **Cross-tabulate.** Make a table with customer role down the side and decision stage across the top, filled with phrase counts. Name the dense cells, and name the empty cells as blind spots.
4. **Map search phrases to customer phrases.** For each search phrase the organization already targets or the suggestions show, list three to five ways real customers say the same thing in conversation. This bridges how people type to a search box and how they talk.
5. **Find interview candidates.** Mark phrases that would make good questions for real conversations (conversational, specific, open to follow-up), scoring 3 strong, 2 weak, 1 skip.
6. **Write the synthesis:**
   - the 10 most useful phrases, with counts and sources
   - the top three customer roles and top three stages
   - what customers say that the organization does not
   - what the organization says that customers never do
   - where evidence is thin and what to collect next
   - a methods note listing sources, dates, and counts
7. **Offer next steps.** Test the best phrases with the persona-jury skill, then with real customers.
