# Customer Research: Start Here

By the end of this kit you will have a small Google Sheet (Google's free spreadsheet app) holding the exact words your customers use, grouped into [themes](glossary.md#theme), plus a repeatable way to check your own wording against those words before you print the flyer, send the newsletter, or rewrite the website. The kit is for a salon owner wondering why nobody books the "color correction" service, a food bank director whose "client choice pantry" sign confuses first-time visitors, and a volunteer treasurer who needs donors to understand what the fund pays for.

Customer research usually sits inside a black box. Agencies sell it as a package, the tools assume you have a research budget, and the people who could explain it charge by the hour. Nothing in it is secret. You read what real people already wrote about you and your neighbors, write down their words, and notice what repeats. This kit shows each step in plain language, and all of it is free.

## What you will have at the end

- A Google Sheet with five [tabs](glossary.md#tab) (separate pages inside one spreadsheet) for your settings, your sources, the quotes you collect, the themes they fall into, and your wording tests.
- A set of [prompts](glossary.md#prompt) (messages you paste into an [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model), such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini) that pull quotes out of reviews, forum threads, and search suggestions, then group them into themes.
- A short list of "customer words" you can use in headlines, signs, and emails, each one traced back to a real quote.
- A way to test two or three versions of your wording on a simulated panel first, then take the winners to real people.

## What a simulated panel can and cannot do

The [simulated respondents](glossary.md#synthetic-respondent) in this kit are an AI model playing the part of a customer. Simulated respondents are good at one thing, which is catching wording that is confusing, cold, or easy to misread before you spend money on it. Customers they are not. Nobody on the panel has been short on rent, missed a pickup window, or squinted at your sign from the parking lot.

Each reaction is a guess assembled from text on the internet, so it can sound sure and be wrong. Treat answers as a [pre-screen](glossary.md#pre-screen) (a quick first filter), never as proof that something works. The final test is always a handful of real customers, and [guide 04](setup/CR-FR-04-test-your-wording.md) says exactly how to do that.

## Who this is for

Anyone who needs to say something to customers, donors, members, or neighbors and wants to say it in their words. You need no technical background and no command line. If you can use a spreadsheet and paste text into a chat window, you can do all of it.

## How long it takes

About an hour for the first setup. After that, a research round on one question usually takes an afternoon.
