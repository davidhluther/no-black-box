# Plain Writing: Start Here

You get a way to write that sounds like a person who knows the subject, and a set of free tools that catch the habits that make it sound like a machine. At the end you'll have a one-page checklist, a short [house style](glossary.md#house-style) for your organization, and a [prompt](glossary.md#prompt) that makes any free AI chat follow your rules. If you want more, you can add programs that check every draft before it goes out.

## The black box

Plenty of tools promise to find or fix "AI-sounding" writing. Most are a black box. You paste text in, you get a score or a rewrite, and nobody tells you why. A consultant can sell you the same style template they sell everyone else. A hair salon writing a newsletter or a food bank writing a grant shouldn't need either.

This kit opens the box. Every rule has its reasoning written next to it. Every check is a short program or a checklist you can read. You decide which rules fit your organization, and you can change any of them.

## Who this is for

- A salon owner who writes a monthly newsletter and wants it to sound like her.
- A food bank volunteer who writes grant text, and a board that wants to trust what it reads.
- A school office that sends a dozen notices a week and has no editor.
- Anyone who uses an [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model) to draft, and keeps getting the same stiff openers and padded endings.

You don't need to know anything technical to use the free version. If a technical word comes up, it's linked to the [glossary](glossary.md) the first time it appears on a page.

## What's in the kit

In every version:

- The style guide, the method, and a way to add your own house style on top.
- A method for checking claims and statistics before they reach a board, a funder, or the public.
- Three writing voices you can ask for by name.
- A method for planning a slide deck as an argument instead of a pile of slides.

Only in the full version, for people comfortable running programs on their own computer:

- Checker programs. One reads a draft and lists every banned word, hollow opener, and copycat sentence shape it finds. One scores a whole folder of writing and compares before and after. One checks finished website text, including menus, titles, and table cells.
- A publishing gate, which is a check that stops a draft from going out if it fails.

If you use the free version, you can ignore this second list. The kit names these programs (article-check, tell-scorer, copy-lint) in the full-version guides.

## What it won't do

It won't promise that a detector will pass your writing, and it won't promise anything about search rankings. The rules are about editorial quality. They help a reader trust what they read. A clean score doesn't prove a person wrote it, and a flag doesn't prove a machine did.
