# House Style Example: Greenfield Pantry (invented)

This is an invented example showing how short a house style can be. Greenfield Pantry is not a real organization.

## Who we are

- We write "Greenfield Pantry" in full on first mention and "the pantry" after that.
- We never say "clients" or "beneficiaries." We say "neighbors" or "families."
- Volunteer job titles are lowercase ("the intake volunteer"), except in a signature.

## Who reads this

- Mostly neighbors using the pantry, and volunteers who sign up for shifts.
- Reading level: plain, short sentences. Many readers use a phone and some read English as a second language.

## Voice

- Warm, plain, direct.
- We would say "We're open Tuesdays and Saturdays." We would not say "Our operational hours are as follows."
- "We" means the pantry's volunteers and staff together.

## Numbers and dates

- Spell out zero through ten, digits from 11 up (the baseline).
- Dates as 10/22/2026. Times as 9 a.m. to noon.
- Money in full: $2,500, not $2.5k.

## Extra rules

- Added to the ban list: "underserved," "food insecure" (say "families who sometimes can't afford groceries"), "needy."
- Every page that names a day or time of day gets checked against the posted schedule before it goes out.
- Link text names the place the link goes ("the volunteer sign-up sheet"), never "here."

## Checks before anything goes out

- The board secretary reads it last.
- Run the word check on every public page. Run the full checker on anything over 500 words.

## Where this came from

- "Underserved" and "needy" came from a neighbor who told us how they read. We stopped using them the same week.
- Schedule check: a flyer went out with last season's hours.
