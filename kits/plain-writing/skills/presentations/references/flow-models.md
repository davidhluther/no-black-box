# Flow Models

Choose a flow model based on the presentation type and where the audience is in the decision cycle.

---

## The Egg-Timer Model (Capabilities / Early-Stage)

Use when: You're early in the relationship. You don't know the client well. The goal is to earn the right to continue the conversation.

The key insight: **The more you talk about the customer with legitimacy, the more you're actually talking about yourself.** Never lead with "about us."

### Structure (20-30 minutes)

| Section | Time | What Happens |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Define Presentation Outcome | 1 min | State the value the audience will get — not your agenda, theirs |
| Describe the Problem | 4 min | Talk about the explicit problem or typical industry problems. This demonstrates expertise without claiming it |
| Explore Implications | 4 min | Amplify the problem — what happens if it's not solved correctly? Negative emotion creates urgency |
| Area of Focus | 2 min | Narrow to 1-3 critical things they need to focus on |
| Present Your Approach | 4 min | NOW talk about yourself — only the capabilities that map directly to the focus areas above |
| Communicate Business Value | 3 min | Use references and case examples — it's too early for specific ROI claims |
| Describe Next Actions | 2 min | Specific, time-bound next steps with owners. Never end with "call me maybe" |

### Why This Works

The typical bad sales presentation goes: My Company → My Product → How It Works → Benefits. This is self-centered and earns no credibility.

The Egg-Timer inverts it: Their World → Their Problem → Their Stakes → Our Relevant Capabilities → Proof → Next Step. You earn the right to talk about yourself by demonstrating you understand them first.

## PIRC Framework (Informative / Internal)

Use when: The goal is to inform, educate, or update. The audience needs to understand something and know what to do with it.

### Structure

**Opening (PIRC):**
- **Purpose:** What is the overarching purpose of this presentation?
- **Importance:** Why does this information matter TO the audience? (Not to you — to them.)
- **Route:** What are the main sections? What order? Give them the map.
- **Clarification:** What concepts or terms need to be defined upfront?

**Body:**
- Point 1: What is it, how will you support it, what makes it memorable?
- Point 2: Same structure.
- Point 3: Same structure.
- Include re-engagement strategies between points (questions, activities, visuals).

**Closing (PINS):**
- **Purpose:** Remind them of the purpose.
- **Importance:** Remind them why it matters to them.
- **Next Steps:** Tell them what to do next. Make it reasonable and doable.
- Signal each transition clearly.

## McKinsey Method (Analytical / Case Submission)

Use when: The audience needs to evaluate an analysis, make a decision based on evidence, or the deck needs to stand alone without a presenter.

### The Rule

Every slide has three layers:

1. **Headline = Assertion.** The slide title states the conclusion or takeaway — not a label. "UNC Printing Services should reorganize to save $925K annually" — not "Analysis of Printing Services."
2. **Subtitle = Key Takeaway.** 1-3 sentences summarizing what the audience should take from this slide.
3. **Body = Evidence.** The data, charts, prose, or visuals that support the assertion. This is where proof lives.

### The Test

Anyone who was NOT in the room should be able to pick up the deck, read it cold, and fully understand the argument by reading only the headlines.

## Solution Presentation (Late-Stage Sales)

Use when: You have deep understanding of the customer's needs. You're presenting a specific solution. The goal is to close or gain commitment.

### Structure

1. **Recap their situation** — Prove you listened. Summarize what you learned in discovery.
2. **State the problem in their language** — Use their words, their metrics, their pain.
3. **Present the solution** — Map directly to the stated problem. No features that don't connect.
4. **Prove it works** — Mini-cases, references, pilots, data. Match proof type to buyer type.
5. **Quantify the value** — ROI, TCO, efficiency gains — in their units and their context.
6. **Propose next steps** — Specific, with owners and dates.

---

## Choosing the Right Model

| Situation | Model |
|-----------|-------|
| First meeting, don't know them well | Egg-Timer |
| Internal update, training, education | PIRC |
| Data-driven analysis, board presentation | McKinsey |
| Deep in sales cycle, presenting a specific proposal | Solution |
| Conference talk, thought leadership | PIRC or Egg-Timer hybrid |

You can combine models. A capabilities deck might use Egg-Timer flow with McKinsey-style assertion headlines. An internal strategy presentation might use PIRC structure with McKinsey evidence slides.
