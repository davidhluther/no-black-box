# Plain Writing Checklist

One page. Copy everything below the line into a new Google Doc (docs.google.com, then Blank document), and print it or keep it open beside your draft. Tick each box before the piece goes out. It takes about ten minutes for a one-page piece. A few terms come up. An AI model (a program like ChatGPT that writes text) may have helped with your draft. Title case (capitalizing the main words, as in How We Changed Our Sign-Up Sheet) is for page titles, and sentence case (capitalizing only the first word and names, as in How we changed our sign-up sheet) is for everything below. An em-dash (the long dash) should be rare. A house style (your organization's own short list of writing rules) sits on top of this checklist.

---

## Plain Writing Checklist

Piece: ______________________  Reader: ______________________  Date: __/__/____

### 1. Before you write (two minutes)

- [ ] I can say who the reader is and what they are trying to do, in one sentence.
- [ ] I know the one thing the piece should leave them able to do or decide.
- [ ] I have named at least one real person, place, program, or source I will use.
- [ ] I know where I will take a position, and one thing I will leave honestly open.

### 2. Words (use Edit, then Find and replace, to search each one)

- [ ] None of these: delve, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, paramount, commence, utilize, facilitate, harness, illuminate, testament, underscore, foster, leverage (as a verb), seamless, streamline, empower, unlock, elevate.
- [ ] Few of these: robust, comprehensive, key, crucial, vital, essential, significant, innovative, navigate, landscape.
- [ ] No Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, That said, At the end of the day.
- [ ] No "In today's ever-evolving world" or any opener like it.
- [ ] I say "AI models," never "AI assistants."

### 3. Sentences

- [ ] No sentence writes about the writing ("In this article," "It's important to note").
- [ ] "Not just X, but Y" and "It's not X, it's Y" appear once at most, including the two-sentence version.
- [ ] No sentence ends on a vague "-ing" tail ("...emphasizing its importance").
- [ ] No "This is where..." pivots.
- [ ] No three sentences in a row start with the same word.
- [ ] I read the piece aloud once. Sentence lengths vary.
- [ ] A series always has "and" or "or" before the last item.

### 4. Structure

- [ ] The ending stops when the point is made. No recap, no "In conclusion," no sentence telling the reader what they learned.
- [ ] At least one section does not end tidy.
- [ ] One section is short and sharp, another is longer.
- [ ] I include one detail only someone who has done the work would know.
- [ ] The piece takes a position somewhere.

### 5. Facts and numbers

- [ ] Every statistic has a named source and a date, and I opened the source myself.
- [ ] If I couldn't verify a number, I said it in words instead.
- [ ] Each statistic appears once.
- [ ] No invented examples dressed up as real ("One company...").
- [ ] Cause-and-effect claims have a reason I can explain, not just two things that happened together.

### 6. Formatting

- [ ] Title in Title Case. Every subheading in sentence case.
- [ ] Straight quotes, no emojis.
- [ ] Em-dashes are rare and have a space on each side.
- [ ] Few colons in the middle of sentences. After a colon, a complete sentence starts with a capital.
- [ ] Numbers: zero through ten spelled out, digits from 11 up. No sentence starts with a numeral.
- [ ] Dates all in the same format.
- [ ] Links use descriptive text. No "click here," no arrows.
- [ ] I kept the headings, lists, and tables. I fixed sentences, not structure.

### 7. Last look

- [ ] Someone who didn't write it has read it.
- [ ] Names, dates, hours, and prices are checked against the real source today.
- [ ] If we have a house style, I read it once more.

---

How to use it. Work top to bottom. When a box doesn't tick, fix that one thing before moving on. Don't try to fix everything at once. If the same box fails three times in a row, that is a habit. Write it into your house style so the next person catches it too.
