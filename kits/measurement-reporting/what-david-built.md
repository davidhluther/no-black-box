# What David Built

This page is for anyone deciding whether to trust the kit, including a hiring manager skimming it. It says what problem David Luther set out to fix, what he made, and what it shows about how he works.

## The problem

A B2B marketing team runs many pages through a CMS and pays for several analytics and search platforms. Leaders ask "how do we know" about every move, and the honest answer is often that each platform says something different. One tool shows organic traffic flat while another shows clicks falling. An AI-visibility platform reports gains that no analytics report can see in visits. A tracking change in the middle of a month looks like a collapse in leads. A monthly deck explains each movement with a confident cause nobody tested, and a pull that quietly failed three weeks ago leaves a chart that looks fine and is not.

## What David built

David Luther ran search and AI visibility measurement for the in-house team at Corpay, and built a working system rather than a better deck. Its parts are the skills in this kit.

- A tracking method that adds events without changing anything that already works, so new measurement never breaks old measurement.
- A number-checking rule that applies to every figure that moves. It defines the measure, tests whether the measurement itself changed, lists the candidate causes and grades each one, and requires the direction to hold on more than one independent platform. When platforms disagree, the report says so and explains why before anyone acts.
- Honest AI-traffic views that are labeled as floors, because most AI-driven visits cannot be told apart from ordinary ones, and no one should quote a share the data cannot give.
- Read-only data pulls with a log that records every scheduled run, so a missed pull is noticed the same day and written into the report instead of hiding behind older data.
- A monthly report format that gives every number a source and a date range, ends every finding with an action and an owner, and passes an evidence review before it reaches leaders.
- A dashboard export that keeps the numbers leaders see in step with the report.

For this public kit David generalized the work so another team can run it with its own platforms, its own CMS and its own cadence.

## What it changed

The team stopped debating whose number was right and started showing how each number was checked. Leaders got reports that answered "why" before they asked, labeled what was a floor and what was a guess, and showed which findings were confirmed on several platforms and which were not. A figure that two platforms disagreed about was held back, with a reason, instead of driving a decision. Quiet failures in scheduled data became visible events with an owner.

## Skills it shows

- Program design, with owners, a cadence and a written map of which sources answer which question.
- Measurement discipline, including defining metrics, separating observation from cause, grading evidence and reconciling independent platforms.
- Automation with safe defaults, such as read-only access, secrets kept out of files, rate limits respected and no silent failures.
- AI-search strategy, including how to measure visibility honestly when the data is partial and the answers vary.
- Executive reporting, where a leader can act on the page without a walkthrough.
- Teaching, which means turning a working method into steps another team can follow.

## The point of view

Analytics gets sold as a black box: a dashboard nobody can question and a cause nobody tested. Knowing how to check a number is not secret knowledge. A team can do it, and every leader reading the report is better served when it does.
