# The metric checklist

Use this list on any report. Items 1, 2 and 5 to 20 apply to every metric. Items 3 and 4 apply whenever search or AI answers could plausibly affect the number (websites, search, AI visibility and leads always qualify). If a question applies and the data is missing, say what is missing and where you looked.

1. Why, not how much. Name the likeliest cause and grade it.
2. Our own work. Did our changes help or hurt? Compare changed things with comparable untouched ones.
3. Rankings and pages. Did rankings drop? One page or group, or our own pages swapping places?
4. AI answers. Are AI summaries or assistants absorbing clicks? Do mentions and citations move differently?
5. Other teams. What did others change in the same period (content, code, campaigns, infrastructure)?
6. Outside forces. Search engine or AI engine updates, market shifts, competitors. Specific to us or everywhere?
7. What to make of it. What it means, what would change the read, what test or fix follows.
8. Discipline. Observation apart from cause. Missing data is missing, not zero. Cite changes by date.
9. Define the measure in plain words.
10. A definition change is never a drop.
11. Name the source and the engine on every figure.
12. Check the measurement holds up (time alignment, same definition, known gaps, what the metric misses).
13. Where money moves, give cost per result before and after.
14. Connect the pieces. What links the pages or campaigns that gained and lost? Recovery or real gain?
15. Act and pre-empt. Each finding ends with an action and an owner. Answer the next question before it is asked. End with a bottom line.
16. Never rest on one platform. A finding seen on one platform only is labeled single-source.
17. Investigate, do not flag. If a check can be done, do it in the same pass.
18. Explain every term of art in one sentence at first use. Every figure carries its date range and data-through date. Label paid and organic.
19. Give the best case for other people's decisions first, then judge.
20. Fold follow-ups back into the report and state corrections.

Every causal claim gets a grade (holds, weakened or refuted) and names its evidence.
