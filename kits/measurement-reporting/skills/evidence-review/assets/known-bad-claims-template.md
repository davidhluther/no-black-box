# Known Bad Claims

A short list of claims your organization has already caught and barred. Any document that contains one is blocked until it is fixed. Keep it forward-looking: each entry is something that went wrong once.

| # | The claim as people tend to write it | Why it is barred | What to say instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (example) "Nine out of ten donors prefer X." | Traces only to a vendor blog with no link to the survey. Tier D. | Cite the survey's own publisher and year, or drop the figure. |
| 2 | (example) "Our program served 5,000 families last year." | The real figure is the count of visits, not families. | "Our program recorded 5,000 visits last year." |

Rules for the list.

1. Match on meaning, not only on the number. Search for the phrasing variants ("nearly all," "roughly nine in ten") as well as the figure.
2. Expect false positives. Clearing them is part of the job, so do not narrow the search to avoid them. A hit that turns out to be a real, sourced figure on another subject is fine. Note why.
3. Add an entry the first time you catch a claim in two or more documents.
4. Date every entry and say who caught it.
