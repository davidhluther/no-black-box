# Board Minutes Style Guide

This is the house style the kit's minutes prompts and skills follow. Change it to match your own board's habits, then paste your version where the prompts say "style guide". Keep it short, because the AI model (a program such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini that reads and writes text) reads all of it every time.

## Voice

- Write in the third person and the past tense. Stay factual and plain.
- Use a person's full name the first time they appear in a section, then their first name or "she" or "he". Skip titles such as "Mr." and "Dr." unless your board uses them.
- If two people share a first name, use the full name for both every time.
- Never put the presenter's name in a heading. Headings name the topic only.
- Write what was decided and why. Leave out jokes, side talk, audio problems, and anything that did not lead to a decision or an update.
- Never invent a name, number, date, or motion. If the transcript (a word-for-word written record of what was said) is unclear, put the unclear part in square brackets, for example "approved [amount unclear] for the repairs", so the secretary can fix it.

## Header

Center the header block.

1. Your mission statement under the words "Our Mission" (optional, and only if your board puts it on its minutes).
2. The name of the board, for example "Example Community Org Board of Directors".
3. "Meeting" and the full date, for example "Meeting - January 21, 2027".
4. The start and end times.
5. The place, such as "In person at the community room and on video".

## Attendance

One list per group, with a bold label. Do not split people on video from people in the room.

- Board members present, with the chair first and marked "(Chair)".
- Advisory members present, only if any attended.
- Staff present.
- Guests, only if any attended.

Do not write an "Absent" list. Minutes record who was there. If the chair was away, say so in the call to order, for example "In the absence of the chair, the vice chair presided." That is board process, and it belongs in the record.

## Call to order

One paragraph. It names who called the meeting to order and whether a quorum was present (a quorum is the minimum number of voting members who must attend for the board's votes to count). If the meeting opened with a reading or a reflection, fold one sentence about it into this paragraph.

## Body

- A top heading "Key Updates and Discussions", then one subheading per topic. Typical topics are Staff Moment, Executive Director's Report, Financial Report, Development Report, Grants Report, Projects and Facilities, and Board and Committee Reports. Add or drop topics to match the agenda.
- Write narrative paragraphs. Put grant amounts, figures, dates, and updates into the sentences. Use a short list only for something that is truly a list, such as a handful of corrections to the previous minutes.
- Describe people we serve in general terms only. No names and no details that could identify anyone.

## Motions and approvals

Give this its own heading near the end.

- List any corrections to the previous minutes first.
- Then write each vote in full. For example, "Approval of Minutes and Reports. Pat Morgan moved to approve the December minutes and accept all reports. Robin Shah seconded. The motion carried unanimously."
- If the vote was not unanimous, give the count. Note any abstentions and any votes against.
- Record every substantive vote, such as a budget, the same way.

## Closing and sign-off

```
Closing
A motion to adjourn was made by [mover] and seconded by [seconder]. The motion carried unanimously, and the meeting was adjourned.

Respectfully submitted by,



[Secretary's name], Secretary
```

## Document format

- Arial, 12 point, 1 inch margins.
- Title block centered. Headings bold.
- A letterhead logo and a signature image are optional. Add a signature image only with the signer's own okay.
- File name pattern, for example `January_21_2027_Board_Meeting_Minutes`.
