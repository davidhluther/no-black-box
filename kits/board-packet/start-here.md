# Start Here With the Board Packet Kit

You get a way to send your board a clean monthly packet, with the last meeting's minutes written from a recording or your notes, a single combined PDF, and a ready-to-review email. The free version uses only Google Workspace and a free [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model) (a program such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini that reads and writes text), and a volunteer who has never written a line of code can set it up. Free setup is four short guides of 10 to 20 minutes each (about 50 minutes if nothing goes wrong), best done over a couple of sittings. A fifth guide, about 45 minutes, runs your first month.

## Why this exists

Plenty of technology is sold as a black box. Someone hands you a finished tool, or an expert applies the same template to every customer, and nobody explains how the thing works. A small nonprofit ends up paying for answers to questions like "how do we get the board its reports on time?" The honest answer is a checklist, a folder layout, and a few prompts you can paste. So here they are, all of it, with the steps written for someone who has never used a command line (a way to run a computer by typing short instructions).

If you picture a hair salon or a small food bank, you have the right reader in mind. They should not have to hire anyone to answer simple questions about the tools that help them serve people.

## Who it's for

A volunteer board secretary or an executive director at a small nonprofit that holds a board meeting each month, circulates reports beforehand, and writes minutes afterward. It assumes you use [Google Workspace for Nonprofits](glossary.md#google-workspace-for-nonprofits) (Gmail, Drive, Docs, and Sheets, free for eligible nonprofits), though a free personal Google account works for most steps.

## What you'll have at the end

With the free version

- A Google Drive folder layout with the board's files in one place and recordings kept private.
- A monthly checklist in a Google Doc.
- A [tracker sheet](glossary.md#tracker-sheet) that lists your reports and people, tells you which reports have not arrived, and has a built-in [check-up](glossary.md#check-up) button.
- Prompts to paste into a free AI model that turn a meeting [transcript](glossary.md#transcript) into minutes, check them against the transcript, and write a [digest](glossary.md#digest) of what the minutes left out.
- A board email template.

The low-cost version adds an [AI Project](glossary.md#ai-project) that remembers your style, a menu in the sheet that makes the month's folder and numbered PDF copies, and a button that writes the board email as a Gmail draft. It never sends. The full version runs the whole cycle for you in [Claude Code](glossary.md#claude-code) and merges the PDF in one step. It is labeled "Claude Code only" because it needs the command line.

[Choose your version](choose-your-version.md) puts the three side by side. You can switch later without starting over.

## Two honest limits

An AI model can invent a name, a number, or a vote. Every version here puts a person between the model's draft and the board, and the prompts include a check step. Skip it and you will eventually send a wrong set of minutes.

Google's Apps Script (the free tool behind the sheet's menus) cannot merge PDFs into one file on its own. The free and low-cost versions show a short manual route, and the full version does it in one step.
