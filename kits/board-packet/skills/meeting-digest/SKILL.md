# Meeting Digest

Works in: Claude Code (a program that runs Claude on your own computer), Claude.ai, ChatGPT

Minutes are short on purpose. They leave out most numbers, dates, and side commitments. A digest keeps those facts in one findable place, so a question like "what did we say about that grant last spring?" has an answer without rereading a two-hour transcript.

## What you need

- The transcript as text. Without it, stop and ask. Do not write a digest from the minutes alone.
- The final minutes, so you can tell what they already carry.
- The meeting date, and the file names of the transcript, any chat log, and the minutes.

Read `references/digest-format.md`, which sits in this folder, first. It holds the exact headings and rules.

## The method

1. Read the transcript through once for the shape of the meeting. If it is long, read in parts and keep notes.
2. Read the final minutes and list what they cover.
3. Go back through the transcript for every decision, motion, number, named funder or partner or vendor, deadline, and promise.
4. Write the five sections in the order the format gives. Quote numbers as heard.
5. Build the "In the transcript but not in the minutes" list by comparing steps 2 and 3. This list is the point of the digest.
6. In the coverage notes, mark every stretch where the transcript is garbled, with the first few words so a person can find it. Mark any place the minutes and transcript seem to disagree, such as a vote the minutes call unanimous when the transcript records a "no".
7. Re-check ten figures at random against the transcript before showing the result.

## Rules

- Never invent anything. Say "unclear" and quote the first words of the passage instead.
- Leave out names and details that could identify any person the organization serves.
- Do not change the minutes. Never backfill them with digest content.
- The digest says on its first lines that it is a derivative index and that the transcript wins.

## Where it goes

Save it next to the transcript in the private transcripts folder, named `YYYY-MM-DD_digest`. Never save it in a shared month folder.
