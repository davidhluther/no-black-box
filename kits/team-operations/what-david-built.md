# What David Built

This page is for hiring managers and for team leads deciding whether the method fits. It describes how David Luther got a marketing and SEO team's recurring work to stay honest, and the rules he wrote down so the team did not have to rely on memory.

## The problem

A B2B marketing team's reporting is a chain. Scheduled pulls feed dashboards. Dashboards feed a weekly or monthly report. The report feeds a leader's decision about budget or priorities. Every link can fail without making noise. A pull dies and an older success makes it look fresh. A report goes out with a number nobody can trace. Someone says a capability "doesn't exist" because a search found nothing. A long AI-assisted session is summarized and loses the decision made an hour earlier. And when a leader asks why a number moved, three tools offer three answers.

At David's employer, Corpay, he ran search, analytics and AI-visibility work through a large number of recurring jobs and AI-assisted sessions across many workstreams. The work was too broad to supervise by watching it. The team needed habits that held up when nobody was looking.

## What David built

He turned each failure into a written rule, then into a small tool that enforces it.

- **A scheduled-job rule.** A missed slot counts as a failure even when old data is still inside its allowed age. Every run writes an outcome, retries happen the same day, and the owner hears about a job only when it is stuck for a reason only they can fix. The rule came from a weekly pull that died quietly while an older success kept the dashboard green. He added a small runner that records, retries and flags.
- **One capability ledger per workstream.** A file says what the workstream can do and what proves it, updated in the same change that ships something. It ended wrong answers of the form "we don't have that," and it recorded what the team had decided against and why.
- **Session briefs and handoffs.** A brief with an unchanging purpose line and a "settled, never re-ask" section, with a limit on how many summaries a session may go through before it hands off. It let work pass between sessions and between people without losing decisions.
- **A workstream inventory.** A fresh scan of every project folder, compared with the written routing notes, so an omission could be told apart from a judgment call.
- **A check on every number before a cause is claimed.** Define the measure, rule out measurement problems, grade each candidate cause, and confirm direction on more than one independent platform. A figure with one source is labeled that way.
- **Data habits.** One script pulls each paid source and everything else reads the saved file. Forecasts are written down before the result, graded afterward, and the misses are published.

## What it changed

The team stopped debating whose dashboard was right, because the report said which sources agreed and which figures rested on one. Quiet failures became visible the same day with the evidence attached. Reports named the slots that had run late. "Can we do that?" was answered from a record that cited its proof. Work moved between people and sessions with a brief stating what was decided, instead of a conversation to reconstruct. None of it required a new platform. It required rules written down, owners named and small tools that checked.

## Skills it shows

- Program design for recurring work, with owners, backups and a defined response to failure.
- **Measurement discipline.** Separating observation from cause, grading explanations, and refusing to rest on one platform.
- Automation that reports honestly on itself, including retry, escalation and delivery of the alert.
- AI-assisted work run as an engineering practice, with briefs, ledgers and handoffs instead of trust.
- Translating all of that into reports a leader can act on, and into plain-language guides a colleague can follow.

## The point of view

Most reliability and measurement practice sits behind a vendor's monitoring product or a consultant's audit. David's view is that these habits are short and teachable, and a team should have them in full. This kit is that view made concrete. Paid help is for fitting the habits to one team's stack and cadence, and it never buys a withheld step.
