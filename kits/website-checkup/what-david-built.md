# What David Built: Website Check-Up

A free, plain-language way for a small business to see its website and local listings the way a stranger does, built from David Luther's professional audit work.

## The problem

Small businesses learn their website is "fine" or "broken" from people who sell the fix. The report arrives as a PDF of jargon, the same template goes to a salon, a clinic, and a contractor, and the owner has no way to tell which findings matter. Meanwhile almost every check in those reports can be done from a stranger's seat with free tools. The black box is the interpretation, not the data.

David Luther ran into the same gap from two sides. At Corpay he works in search and content strategy for a large business-payments company, where technical audits, competitor comparisons, page-speed checks, and structured-data work have to be repeatable and every number has to name its source. Through Pigasus Group he does the same work for small local businesses, where the customer is an owner with an afternoon, not a marketing department.

## What David made

He turned both streams of work into one kit. The center of it is an [outside-in](glossary.md#outside-in) [scorecard](glossary.md#scorecard). It looks at a business's Google listing, reviews, page speed, [structured data](glossary.md#structured-data), local search presence, and positioning, scores each check as pass, partial, fail, or unknown, and compares the business with its neighbors. Unknown is a first-class answer. A check that couldn't be done is listed at the end of the report as something the method could not see, so a missing number never turns into a guess.

Around the scorecard sit the supporting [skills](glossary.md#skill), each one a saved set of instructions for a single job.

- A deeper local-search check and a structured-data writer.
- Keyword and question discovery, and a market analysis built from three research angles.
- A technical audit that scores each finding by search impact, business impact, and effort.
- Checks for pages that compete with each other, a merge plan, and an internal-links audit.
- Page speed, titles and descriptions, a [backlink](glossary.md#backlink) check, a blog index, and a crawler-access check.
- An action plan that ranks everything.

A short marketing [baseline](glossary.md#baseline) checklist sits underneath the scorecard. It lists the things a business should own and have in place before it pays for ads or content, from one consistent web address to a monthly fifteen-minute habit of reading the numbers.

He rewrote the originals, which were built for a corporate content team and for a consultancy's pipeline, so that an owner can use them without a developer. The free version runs on Google's and Microsoft's free reports, a [Google Sheet](glossary.md#google-sheet), and [prompts](glossary.md#prompt) pasted into any free AI chat. Low cost adds a saved workspace and a monthly automatic speed check. A full version adds scripts, a site [crawl](glossary.md#crawl), and an optional paid link database. Only one skill truly needs paid data, the competitor link comparison. It is marked full-only, and the free substitute is named beside it.

## What it changed

A business owner can find out in an afternoon how they look to a stranger, and get a ranked list of fixes that says what each change will and won't do. It refuses to promise rankings, and it treats a wrong address or wrong hours shown to customers as the most urgent problem in the plan. A check-up button inside the sheet gives a stuck user one [support code](glossary.md#support-code) that points to the exact step that failed, which makes remote help quick and keeps customer data out of the process.

## Skills it shows

- Turning expert audit work into plain language for non-experts, with a glossary and step-by-step guides.
- Evidence discipline. Every finding carries its source and a confidence label, hypotheses are kept apart from tasks, and unknowns are reported honestly.
- Local search, structured data, technical [SEO](glossary.md#seo), page speed, and competitor analysis.
- Designing across three tiers so the free version stands on its own.
- Building small, safe tools. A Google [Apps Script](glossary.md#apps-script) check-up that never reads or reports cell contents, and read-only scripts that need no secrets.
- Prioritization under limits. Impact, urgency, and effort sized for a one-person business.
- Writing for a mixed audience, with a recruiter's skim and an owner's careful read in mind.
