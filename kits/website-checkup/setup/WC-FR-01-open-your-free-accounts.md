# WC-FR-01: Open Your Free Accounts

You will set up three free reports that show how Google and Bing see your site, and check that your business has a Google listing. About 30 minutes, plus a wait of a few days before real numbers appear.

If you get stuck, the check-up button in your sheet prints a [support code](../glossary.md#support-code), a short code that tells a helper which step failed. The box at the bottom says where to send it.

You need your own website address (for example https://www.example.org) and the right to change something on your site or its address records. If a web person manages your site, you can send them step 3 and carry on.

1. **WC-FR-01-1.** Sign in to your Google account at accounts.google.com. Use the account that owns your business listing if you know which it is.
   You'll know it worked when you see your name or email at the top right of any Google page. If you land in a sign-in loop, or Google asks for a code you don't receive, it's E01.

2. **WC-FR-01-2.** Open [Search Console](../glossary.md#search-console) at search.google.com/search-console and choose "Add property". You'll see two choices.
   - "[Domain](../glossary.md#domain)" covers every version of your address, and needs you to edit your [DNS](../glossary.md#dns) records.
   - "URL prefix" covers one exact address and has easier proofs. If you aren't sure, choose URL prefix and type your full address starting with https://.
   You'll know it worked when Google asks you to prove you own the site. If it says the address isn't valid, it's E03.

3. **WC-FR-01-3.** Prove ownership. Pick the method Google suggests first, and follow its steps. For a URL prefix, the "HTML tag" method means pasting one line into your site's settings, which most site builders (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress) have a box for. If you can't do it, forward the page to your web person.
   You'll know it worked when Google says "Ownership verified". If the check fails after you've made the change, wait ten minutes and try again. Still failing is E03.

4. **WC-FR-01-4.** Give Google a list of your pages. This list is called a [sitemap](../glossary.md#sitemap), and most sites already have one. In Search Console, open "Sitemaps" in the left menu, type `sitemap.xml` in the box, and click Submit.
   You'll know it worked when the status says "Success". "Couldn't fetch" often clears in a day. If it stays, it's E02.

5. **WC-FR-01-5.** Open [Bing Webmaster Tools](../glossary.md#bing-webmaster-tools) at bing.com/webmasters and sign in with a Microsoft account (make a free one if you need it). Choose to import your site from Google Search Console and allow access.
   You'll know it worked when your site appears in the Bing list. If the import button is greyed out or fails, it's E04.

6. **WC-FR-01-6.** Find your [Google Business Profile](../glossary.md#google-business-profile). Open maps.google.com, type your business name and town, and open your listing. Look for a link that says "Claim this business". If you see it, nobody has verified the listing yet. Write down yes or no.
   You'll know it worked when you can see your business on the map and know whether it's claimed. If you can't find it at all, it's E13.

7. **WC-FR-01-7.** Write today's date on a note and set a reminder for three days from now. Search Console and Bing begin showing data only after a few days.
   You'll know it worked when the reminder is set. If Search Console says "No data available" on day one, that's normal, and it's E02 only if it still says that after a week.

> **How to get help.** Write down the step ID where you got stuck (it looks like WC-FR-01-3). If your kit has a check-up button or command, run it and copy the support code it prints. Then send both to Pigasus Group at https://pigasus.group/contact. Setup questions are free.
