# Website Check-Up

**What you get.** A plain [scorecard](glossary.md#scorecard) showing how your business looks to a stranger who searches for it, plus the five fixes that matter most, in order. It covers your [Google Business Profile](glossary.md#google-business-profile), your reviews, how fast your site loads, the labels behind your pages that search engines read, and how you compare with the businesses down the road. It costs nothing to run the free version, and you finish your first scorecard in an afternoon.

## The black box, and the way out

Most owners learn how their website is doing by paying someone to tell them. The answer comes back as a report full of terms nobody explains, and the same report goes to every customer. A hair salon or a small food bank has no reason to buy that. Nearly every check in it can be done with free tools from Google and Microsoft, plus an [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model) to help read the results. This kit shows you which checks matter, how to run them, and how to read what you find.

## Who it's for

- A business with a website and a local customer base: a salon, a restaurant, a contractor, a clinic, a shop, a nonprofit with a front door.
- Someone who handles their own marketing, or who hired a web person and wants to understand the work.
- A volunteer or an office manager with an hour to spare and no technical background.

## What you will have at the end

1. A [Google Sheet](glossary.md#google-sheet) with a scorecard. Each check gets a result (pass, partial, fail, or unknown), and the sheet adds up the points by section.
2. A list of the 16 basics every business should have in place before spending on ads or content, with a mark beside each one.
3. A short action plan: what to do this week, this month, and later.
4. A one-page monthly habit, so you notice when something breaks.
5. A "Kit help" menu in the sheet. One click runs a check-up of the sheet itself and prints a [support code](glossary.md#support-code) you can send if you get stuck.

## What it checks

It looks at your business from the outside, the way a customer would. The method is called [outside-in](glossary.md#outside-in), which means it needs no logins to your accounts.

- Your Google listing: name, category, address or service area, hours, description, photos.
- Reviews, compared with nearby competitors.
- Speed, using [PageSpeed Insights](glossary.md#pagespeed-insights) and the [Chrome UX Report](glossary.md#chrome-ux-report), two free Google tools.
- [Structured data](glossary.md#structured-data), the hidden labels that tell search engines your business name and address.
- Local search: whether your name, address, and phone number match everywhere, and whether your site names your town.
- Positioning: whether your site says who you serve and what only you can claim.
- Search visibility, using [Search Console](glossary.md#search-console) and [Bing Webmaster Tools](glossary.md#bing-webmaster-tools), both free for site owners.

## What it can't see

Anything behind a login is outside its reach, including your listing's private dashboard, your site's visitor counts, and what your customers search from their own phones. Each report ends with a plain list of what could not be checked, so a missing number never turns into a guess.
