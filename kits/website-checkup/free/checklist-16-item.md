# The 16-item marketing baseline checklist

Paste this into a Google Doc, or tick the boxes in the Baseline16 tab of your sheet. For each item, write Done, Needs done, or Not applicable, and where you looked. Almost all of it costs time, not money. Each item says how to check it.

## Phase 1: Foundations (own your ground)

- [ ] 1. One web address, everywhere. Type both the www and no-www versions into a browser. One should forward to the other.
- [ ] 2. A phone number you can keep forever. It belongs to the business, not to a person or provider who could take it back.
- [ ] 3. Email on your own [domain](../glossary.md#domain) that delivers. Send a test to a free email account and check it reaches the inbox, not spam.
- [ ] 4. A written list of who owns what. Where the web address was bought, who runs the address book that points it to your site ([DNS](../glossary.md#dns)), who hosts the site, who owns the Google listing, the social accounts, and the analytics. Recovery details written down.

## Phase 2: Presence (be findable)

- [ ] 5. [Google Business Profile](../glossary.md#google-business-profile) claimed and filled in honestly.
- [ ] 6. Social profiles claimed, with the same name and details.
- [ ] 7. Listings beyond Google: Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, your chamber of commerce. Same name, number, and address everywhere.
- [ ] 8. Real photos of the place, the people, or the work. No stock photos.
- [ ] 9. A messaging kit: one short description of the business, written to fit each platform's limits, plus images in each platform's sizes.

## Phase 3: Paths in (be reachable)

- [ ] 10. Forms that deliver. Send a test message through every form, every month, and confirm it arrives.
- [ ] 11. A booking path. A customer can get on your calendar at 9 p.m. without a phone call.

## Phase 4: Instruments (know what's happening)

- [ ] 12. [Search Console](../glossary.md#search-console) and [Bing Webmaster Tools](../glossary.md#bing-webmaster-tools) set up, plus a visit counter such as Google Analytics or Microsoft Clarity.
- [ ] 13. You have asked ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini what your business does and written down anything wrong.
- [ ] 14. A review flow: you ask, there's a short link, and every review gets a reply.
- [ ] 15. You have looked at who links to your site (Search Console, Links report).
- [ ] 16. A monthly 15-minute reading: did anything break, what did search send, where did people struggle.

## Four small items

- [ ] A favicon (the small icon in the browser tab)
- [ ] Share images (the picture shown when someone posts a link to your site)
- [ ] A web manifest (a small file that describes your site to phones)
- [ ] HTTPS (the padlock in the address bar)

## Storefront and no-storefront notes

If you have a public door, publish your address and real hours, and show the front, the inside, and the people. If you work by appointment or go to customers, verify the Google listing with a real address but hide it, name your service areas (counties beat towns), set appointment-required instead of making up hours, and show yourself, the work, and the places it happens. The booking path counts double when there is no walk-in door.
