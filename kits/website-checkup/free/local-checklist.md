# Local listing checklist, by hand

For checking your Google listing and local search yourself, with no tools beyond a browser. About 45 minutes.

## Part 1: Google listing (search your business name in Google Maps)

- [ ] It appears, and there is no "Claim this business" link.
- [ ] The name is exactly your business name. No city or service words added. (Keyword-stuffed names are one of the most common reasons listings get suspended.)
- [ ] The main category names your core service. Compare with your three closest competitors. If theirs is more specific, note it.
- [ ] Extra categories cover other services you really sell. None for things you don't.
- [ ] Address. Storefront: shown. No public door: hidden, with service areas listed.
- [ ] Hours match reality. If you work by appointment, hours are blank and the appointment attribute is set, rather than hours that claim someone answers.
- [ ] The website link goes to your one official address, with no tracking tags on the end.
- [ ] A description that says what you do, who you serve, and where. No web addresses, prices, or promotions in it.
- [ ] Services or products are listed with a line each.
- [ ] Photos are real and recent.
- [ ] Any post, photo, or answered question in the last 90 days.

Choosing a category: pick the one that matches what you are, not the one with the most searches, and let extra categories catch the neighbors. The main category can be changed later without re-verifying. Changing the name or address can send you back through verification, so settle those first.

## Part 2: Reviews

- [ ] Write down your rating and count, and the same for three to five local competitors.
- [ ] Read the newest ten reviews. Are they recent? Did you reply?
- [ ] Is there a second review site that matters for your kind of business?
- [ ] Do you have a short link to your review page, and do you ask?

## Part 4: Your website's local pages

- [ ] Your town or area appears in the page title, the main heading, and the body text of your key pages.
- [ ] Each major service has its own page.
- [ ] A map, directions, or a clear statement of the area you serve.
- [ ] Your name, address, and phone on the page, matching Google.
- [ ] Local proof: local reviews, photos, mentions of the community.

## Part 5: Three searches

Run each in a private window and note where your business appears.

1. [your main service] near me
2. [your main service] [your town]
3. [your business name]

Results change with where you stand. Take them as a rough guide, and repeat on your phone while standing in the area you serve if it matters.
