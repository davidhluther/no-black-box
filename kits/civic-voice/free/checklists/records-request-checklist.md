# Records request checklist

Copy into a Google Doc. Dates are mm/dd/yyyy.

## 1. Decide

- [ ] Who is asking: me or my group? (Keep it the same through the whole request.)
- [ ] Which agency holds the records? (If unsure, start with its [clerk](../../glossary.md#clerk) or records officer.)
- [ ] What do I want to understand, in one sentence?
- [ ] Which documents would show it? (Reports, emails, contracts, maps, permits, meeting recordings.)

## 2. Look up your state's law

Never use a citation or deadline from an [AI model](../../glossary.md#ai-model) without checking it on an official page. Write the link and date for each row. Your search steps are in the kit's [skill](../../glossary.md#skill) `records-request`, file `references/state-law-lookup.md`.

## 3. Draft and review

- [ ] Every item is a record, not a question.
- [ ] Each item has a record type, subject, role or office, and date range.
- [ ] No request for private people's personal details.
- [ ] Electronic copies requested. Fee estimate requested above $____.
- [ ] Any denial must cite the [exemption](../../glossary.md#exemption) and release the rest.
- [ ] Offer to narrow. Contact details right.
- [ ] Tone is neutral. One page if possible.

## 4. Send and track

- [ ] Send by a method that leaves a dated record (online form, email, or certified mail).
- [ ] Add a row to the Records requests tab with the response due date and a follow-up date.
- [ ] Put the due date on your calendar.

## 5. When the date passes

- [ ] No response: send the polite follow-up from the template.
- [ ] Fee estimate: reply to confirm or narrow.
- [ ] Partial release or denial: ask for the exemption cited, and the review steps. Call your state's open-government help line or a legal aid group before appealing.

## 6. When records arrive

- [ ] Save them unchanged in a dated folder.
- [ ] Note what was provided and what was not, item by item.
- [ ] Remember that what you received tells you what the office sent, not whether other records exist.
