# CV-FR-02: Prepare for a Meeting and Speak

Free version, guide 2. You will save a meeting's packet, write a dated prep memo, write a comment that fits your time limit, deliver it, and record what happened. Plan about two hours spread over the week before the meeting. You need your tracker from [CV-FR-01](CV-FR-01-make-your-tracker.md) and a free [AI model](../glossary.md#ai-model) such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. The method is the same whatever your view on the item. You decide what to ask for.

1. **CV-FR-02-1.** In Google Drive, make a folder named "Civic voice", with a "Meetings" folder inside it. For this meeting, make a folder inside Meetings named with the date and the body, like "2027-03-09-example-town-planning-board", and a folder named "source" inside that one.
   You'll know it worked when you can see the three nested folders in Drive.

2. **CV-FR-02-2.** Go to the body's own agenda page (the town or county website). Download the agenda and the full [agenda packet](../glossary.md#agenda-packet) (the bundle of reports and drafts the members read). Put them in the "source" folder without editing them. Open a new Google Doc in the meeting folder called "source note" and write where you got the files, the date and time, and the page count.
   You'll know it worked when the source folder holds the agenda and packet and the note says where they came from. If you can open the PDF but cannot select any text with your cursor, the file is a scan, and it's E09.

3. **CV-FR-02-3.** Find the [public comment](../glossary.md#public-comment) rules on the body's website or ask the [clerk](../glossary.md#clerk). Write down the sign-up deadline, the time limit per speaker, which items you may speak on, and whether written comments are accepted, with the link beside each.
   You'll know it worked when you have four answers, or you have written "not found, asked the clerk on mm/dd/yyyy." If you cannot find them, it's E10.

4. **CV-FR-02-4.** In your tracker, add a row to the Meetings tab with the meeting date, body, item number, topic, the packet link, the date you downloaded it, and the sign-up deadline.
   You'll know it worked when the row is filled in across.

5. **CV-FR-02-5.** Get the packet's text. Open the PDF, select the pages for the one or two agenda items you care about (plus the first page that lists the agenda), and copy them. Work one agenda item at a time. Free AI models limit how much you can paste at once.
   You'll know it worked when you have copied text in your clipboard with the page markers still in it. If the chat says the text is too long, copy fewer pages (it's E09).

6. **CV-FR-02-6.** Open the file `free/prompts.md`. Copy the "Rules block," then "Prep memo from a meeting packet" ([prompt](../glossary.md#prompt) 1). Paste both into the AI model, fill in the brackets, and paste the packet text at the end. Send it. Copy the answer into a Google Doc in the meeting folder named "prep memo yyyy-mm-dd."
   You'll know it worked when the memo has a ranked table, a fact table with packet pages for each fact, and a "check before you speak" list.

7. **CV-FR-02-7.** Check the facts you plan to say out loud. For each, open the packet to the page the memo names, and find the fact. Tick it off. Delete or mark "unverified" anything you cannot find on that page.
   You'll know it worked when every fact you plan to use is ticked or removed. If the memo cites a page that does not hold the fact, or invents a number, quote, or law, it's E08.

8. **CV-FR-02-8.** If you plan to ask the body for something (a change, a condition, a standard), find the governing document, such as the zoning [ordinance](../glossary.md#ordinance) or policy, from the body's website. Paste the matching sections, plus your list of asks, into prompt 2 ("Check my ask against the governing document"). Note which asks the document already requires.
   You'll know it worked when each ask is labeled as already required, conflicting, or not covered. If you cannot find the document, write "not checked" in your memo.

9. **CV-FR-02-9.** Write your comment. Use prompt 3, with the time limit from step 3 and one sentence of your own about why this matters to you. Read the spoken version aloud with a timer. Cut it until it runs about 15 seconds under the limit.
   You'll know it worked when your aloud reading fits the time limit with a little room. If it runs long, it's E11.

10. **CV-FR-02-10.** Add a row to the Comments tab with the meeting date, body, item number, and how you are speaking (as a resident or for a group). Print two copies of your comment, and bring one more handout if you have one.
    You'll know it worked when you have the printed pages and the row.

11. **CV-FR-02-11.** Sign up by the deadline using the method the body requires. On the day, arrive early, give the clerk a copy if they ask, and speak to the chair. If the meeting runs long or your item moves and you cannot speak, send the written version to the clerk the same day and ask how to have it added to the record. Rules differ.
    You'll know it worked when you have either spoken or sent the written copy. If you cannot find where to send it, call or email the clerk's office.

12. **CV-FR-02-12.** Within a day, run prompt 4 ("Record the outcome") with your notes. Save it in the meeting folder as "outcome yyyy-mm-dd" and say at the top whether it comes from your notes or [adopted minutes](../glossary.md#adopted-minutes). Update the Meetings and Comments rows. When the minutes are posted, read them and correct your record.
    You'll know it worked when the outcome record is saved and the rows show the result.

The checklist for this whole guide is `free/checklists/meeting-prep-checklist.md`.
