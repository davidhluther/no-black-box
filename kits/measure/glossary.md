# Glossary

Every technical word used in this kit, in plain words. Terms are listed alphabetically.

**AI referral traffic.** Visits to your site that arrive by clicking a link inside an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude or Copilot. It is a floor, not the total, because many AI answers send visitors with no sign of where they came from.

**API.** A way for two programs to talk, like a waiter carrying orders between you and the kitchen. A program asks for numbers, and the service sends them back.

**Apps Script.** Google's free tool for adding small programs to a Google Sheet. It lives under Extensions in the sheet's menu and adds things like a Kit help menu or a button that pulls numbers in.

**channel group.** A way of sorting visits into labeled buckets such as Organic Search, Direct, Email and Referral. A custom channel group is your own set of buckets, for example one called AI Traffic.

**click-through rate.** The share of views that became clicks. If a search result was shown 100 times and clicked five times, the rate is five percent.

**Clarity.** Microsoft Clarity, a free tool that shows how visitors use a site: where they click, how far they scroll, where they get stuck.

**container.** In Google Tag Manager, the box that holds all of one site's tracking pieces. Its name looks like GTM-XXXXXXX.

**CSV.** A plain table saved as text, one row per line with commas between the columns. Any spreadsheet can open it.

**data layer.** A short list a website keeps in the background to announce things that happened, such as "a form was sent". Google Tag Manager listens to it.

**environment variable.** A named setting your computer holds for programs to read, like a label on a locker. A program asks for the label by name, so a secret never has to be written in a file.

**event.** One thing Google Analytics 4 records, such as a page view, a click on a download link or a form being sent. It has a name.

**GA4.** Google Analytics 4, Google's free tool that counts visits to a website and what visitors do there.

**Google Tag Manager.** A free Google tool where you add and change tracking pieces for a website without editing the site itself. People call it Tag Manager.

**impression.** One time your page was shown in a search result, whether or not anyone clicked.

**key event.** An event you tell Google Analytics 4 counts as a goal reached, such as a completed contact form. GA4 used to call this a conversion.

**Looker Studio.** Google's free dashboard tool. It draws charts from a Google Sheet or other data source and updates when the data does.

**parameter.** An extra labeled fact attached to an event, such as which form was sent or which file was downloaded.

**preview mode.** The Preview button in Google Tag Manager. It opens your real site with your draft changes loaded, for you only, so you can test before anyone else sees them.

**publish.** In Google Tag Manager, making your draft changes live for every visitor. Nothing you do before this step reaches the public.

**referrer.** The address of the page a visitor came from. When a visitor clicks a link in an AI tool, the referrer is usually that tool's website, if it is passed along at all.

**regex.** Short for regular expression, a compact pattern for matching text, like a stencil. One pattern can match a whole list of website names.

**Search Console.** Google Search Console, a free report of how your site appears in Google search: which searches showed it, how often, and how many people clicked.

**service account.** A login that belongs to a program instead of a person. You give it permission to read your data, and it can do nothing else.

**session.** One visit to your site, from arriving to leaving or going quiet. An engaged session is a visit that lasted at least ten seconds, had a key event or viewed at least two pages.

**single-source.** A figure confirmed on only one platform. Reports label it that way and avoid acting on it until another independent source agrees.

**skill.** A folder of written instructions, and sometimes small programs, that an AI tool such as Claude can follow to do one job the same way each time.

**tag.** In Google Tag Manager, one instruction to send a piece of information, such as "tell GA4 a download was clicked".

**token.** A long secret code that proves a program may read your data, like a key card. Treat it like a password.

**trigger.** In Google Tag Manager, the rule that says when a tag fires, such as "when someone clicks a link ending in .pdf".

**variable.** In Google Tag Manager, a named value a tag or trigger can use, such as the address of the link that was clicked.

**workspace.** In Google Tag Manager, a draft copy where you make changes. Several drafts can exist side by side, and nothing in one reaches your visitors until it is published.
