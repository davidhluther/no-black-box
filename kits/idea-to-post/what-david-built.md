# What David Built

This page is for recruiters, clients, and curious neighbors. It says what problem David Luther set out to solve, what he made, and what it shows about how he works.

## The problem

Publishing a good article takes many hands and many small checks. Someone finds a topic. Someone else discovers it is already covered. A research brief gets written, then a draft.

Then an editor catches invented statistics or a voice that sounds like a brochure. Images are late. The post goes live and nobody looks at it again until a manager asks why traffic is down. Each stage lives in a different tool, and the knowledge of how to do it well sits in a few people's heads.

For a large company that costs time and rework. For a small organization it usually means the blog never happens, or gets handed to an agency that sells the same package to everyone.

## What David built

David Luther built the whole path as a set of small, separate [skills](glossary.md#skill), each one doing a single job and handing a file to the next, with a person making every judgment call that matters. While running search and content work at Corpay, he found that the expensive failures came from missing gates rather than missing effort. So he built the gates.

- **A cheap screen before the expensive stage.** Topics are checked against what already exists, against each other, and against a minimum level of demand before anyone writes a brief. It reports what it could not check instead of calling it clean.
- **One author for briefs.** A single skill writes the brief, with a required verdict (new post, [refresh](glossary.md#refresh), hold, or skip), a check for overlapping content, and statistics that must trace to a named source. The drafting skills refuse to start without a complete brief.
- **Drafting that develops the plan.** The draft follows the brief's outline in full, uses only the facts the brief supplies, and runs through quality checks for invented claims, filler words, and structure.
- **Honest sources.** Weekly idea gathering reads the original study or announcement, not just a newsletter's summary, records where summaries drifted from the source, and grades every item by how far it can be trusted.
- **A publish step that stops for a person.** It starts with a practice run and never publishes from a schedule.
- **Measurement and refresh.** Posts are checked at set points afterward, with a rule that an update must change something real, never only the date.
- **Failures are written down.** Every skill that cannot read a source says so, so a quiet result is never mistaken for a clean one.

Then he rewrote it for people with no technical background: a free version built on Google Sheets and a pasted [prompt](glossary.md#prompt) for each step, a low-cost version that adds one AI plan and small scripts, and a full version that runs in [Claude Code](glossary.md#claude-code). A volunteer can follow the free version with a checklist, and a team can run the full version with checks automated.

## What it changed

Work that used to depend on a few experienced people became a repeatable process that a newcomer can follow and that leaves a record of what was decided and why. Duplicate and low-value topics are stopped earlier. Unsupported statistics are caught before publication. Posts get measured and either updated or left alone on purpose. For small organizations, the same method is open, free to use, and does not need an expensive tool or an outside expert to answer basic questions.

## Skills this shows

- Designing a multi-stage workflow with clear handoffs between stages.
- Building quality gates and failure reporting so errors surface instead of hiding.
- Search and AI-answer strategy: intent, structure, and measurement without overpromising.
- Research method: source tracing, confidence grading, and the discipline of testing claims before repeating them.
- Writing systems: a style guide turned into checks a program can run.
- Writing technical help for people who have never used a [command line](glossary.md#command-line), with [step IDs](glossary.md#step-id), problem codes, and a support check built in.
- Practical programming in [Python](glossary.md#python) and Google [Apps Script](glossary.md#apps-script), with read-only access to other people's data by default.
- Deciding what to automate and what a person must always decide.

## Why it is open

David's view is that much of how technology and business works is held in a black box, partly because it is complicated and partly because people benefit from selling the knowledge back to you. There is no reason a hair salon or a small food bank should have to pay someone to answer simple questions about the technology that serves their community. This kit explains each step so anyone can use it, and the paid offer is limited to David's time fitting it to one organization. It never buys withheld information.
