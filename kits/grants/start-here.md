# Grants and Giving: Start Here

You will end up with a working grants tracker in Google Sheets (Google's free spreadsheet app), a way to research each funder (a person, foundation, company or government office that gives money) from its own public records, a method for writing grant drafts in which every number traces to a source, and ready-to-use pages and letters for the ways of giving most small nonprofits miss. You never type commands, but you will paste one small program into Google Sheets twice. The free version is several short guides, about an hour each at most, which a volunteer with no technical background can do over a few sittings. It has no automatic weekly search. You run that search by hand, and the automatic one comes with the low-cost version.

## The black box

Finding grants is usually sold as a mystery. Consultants and paid databases make it sound as if you need a special list, a special login or a special person. You don't. Private foundations (charities whose job is giving money away) must file a yearly tax return, called a Form 990-PF (a form that lists every grant the foundation paid), and it is public. A foundation's real giving habits are written there. The rest of this kit is a plain way to use that, keep your own records straight, and write drafts you can stand behind.

## Who it is for

Picture a small food bank, a youth program, a housing group or an animal shelter. One staff member or one volunteer is in charge of grants, along with six other jobs. The board asks "what are we applying for?" and nobody can answer in one sentence. This kit is for that person. A hair salon owner who wants to know whether their community fund would help them will find the same ideas work.

## What you will have at the end

1. **A tracker.** A Google Sheet with tabs for Settings, Pipeline (every ask, past and planned), Funders (what you learned about each one), Claims (every fact you may state about your organization, each with its source), Dashboard (win rate, dollars awarded, open asks, deadlines), Rubric and DraftCheck.
2. **A [check-up](glossary.md#check-up)** (a Help menu button that tests the setup). It prints one short support code (a code that tells a helper which step you were on and what went wrong). The code holds none of your information.
3. **Paste-in prompts** (messages you paste into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini). They help you read a funder's guidelines, build your [claims register](glossary.md#claims-register), draft a letter of inquiry (a short first letter some funders ask for), and review it with fresh eyes.
4. **A draft checker.** It lists every number in a draft that does not match a verified claim.
5. **Checklists and a board page.** Funder research, before you submit, giving channels, and a one-page set of approval rules for the board.
6. **A giving-channels pack.** Page text and donor letters for gifts from a donor-advised fund (a charitable account a donor holds at a sponsor), employer matching gifts (a gift where an employer matches an employee's own donation), monthly giving, gifts of stock and from an IRA, and gifts in a will.

The low-cost version adds a weekly search of Grants.gov (the US government's public list of grant opportunities) that fills a tab in your sheet and emails you if it fails. The full version adds the research and drafting skills (folders of written instructions that a chat program can follow) for Claude Code (a program that lets Claude work on your computer's files) and a one-command build of the tracker, a phone-friendly page and funder files. [Choose your version](choose-your-version.md) puts the three side by side.

## What it won't do

- It won't invent funders. AI models (programs that read and write text) make things up fluently, so every [prompt](glossary.md#prompt) in this kit asks the model to work only from text you paste in, and every fact gets a source and a date.
- It won't send anything. Drafts are for a person to review. Nothing goes to a funder, and no accounts are created for you.
- It won't promise you money. It makes your search and your records better. Funders still decide.

## How to begin

1. Read [choose your version](choose-your-version.md). Most readers start free.
2. Look at the [shopping list](shopping-list.md) to see what you need and what is optional. Most items cost nothing.
3. Open `setup/GR-FR-01-make-your-tracker.md` and follow the numbered steps. Its first steps show how to download the kit's files from GitHub (a website where people share project files) at https://github.com/davidhluther/no-black-box (the shared [get the kit files](../../guides/get-the-files.md) guide covers Windows, Mac and Chromebook). Each step says how you will know it worked and what to do if it did not.

Words you don't know are explained in the [glossary](glossary.md), and again the first time they appear on each page.
