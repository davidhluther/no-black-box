# Building a claims register

A claims register is a spreadsheet of every fact your drafts may state about your organization, each with its source. It stops the most common grant-writing mistake, which is a number that was true two years ago or was never written down anywhere.

## Columns

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| id | A short code by topic and number: ID-01 for identity, PRG-01 for programs, FIN-01 for money, GOV-01 for board and governance. Two to eight capital letters, a hyphen, two or three digits. |
| claim | The fact in a sentence, written so it could go in a draft almost as is. |
| value | The one number in the claim, written as it appears in drafts (1200, or 35 for 35 percent). Leave blank if the claim has no number. |
| as_of | The period the number covers: "fiscal year 2025," "as of 09/30/2026," "current." |
| source | Where a person can check it: document name and page, or a web address with the date checked. |
| status | source-verified, external-verified, unverified, conflict or do-not-use. |

## Where the facts come from

In this order of trust: audited financial statements, the IRS determination letter, the annual report, board-approved minutes, the organization's own site, then outside sites such as ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer or Candid (those are external-verified, with the date checked). A spoken figure from a staff member is unverified until it is written down somewhere.

## Habits that keep it honest

- One number per row. "Served 1,200 households and distributed 240,000 pounds" is two rows.
- Keep the period with the number. A grant reader will ask "which year?"
- When two sources disagree, add a conflict row that says which figure to use and why.
- Retire a claim by changing its status to do-not-use. Never delete it, so the old number cannot sneak back in under a new ID.
- Review the register after each annual report, audit or new program.

## Boilerplate and programs

Reusable text goes in two more files. Boilerplate holds organization descriptions at three lengths (about 50, 150 and 300 words), history, need, capacity, evaluation and sustainability, each block listing the claim IDs it uses. A programs file has one section per program: what it does, who it serves, the claim IDs to use and which funder priorities it answers. Keep both short and cite IDs instead of repeating numbers.
