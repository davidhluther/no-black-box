# Start Here for the Continuity Kit

You will learn one number, from zero to 15, that says how well your business could run if you disappeared tomorrow. Then you get three fill-in [runbooks](glossary.md#runbook), for money, daily operations and access, that raise that number. A second person can follow them without calling you.

It is for the owner of a small shop or practice, the coordinator of a food pantry, a volunteer treasurer, or anyone who is the only person who knows how something works. A board member reading it will recognize the question, which is who else could do this.

## What you will have at the end

- A [bus test](glossary.md#bus-test) score out of 15 across five functions of your business, with the weakest and the strongest named.
- A short written note on what would stop first in each function and how soon it would hurt.
- Three runbooks, written in your words, each ending in a [two-week test](glossary.md#two-week-test) that someone else runs.
- A Google Sheet that keeps the score and a [check-up](glossary.md#check-up) button that tells you if it is set up right.
- A date on the calendar to score again.

## The black box, once

When people ask for this kind of plan, they usually hire someone to interview them, write a binder and hand over a template. The binder helps, but it often sits unopened because it was built for a generic company rather than for the way your business works. The method itself is simple, short and fits on a page. This kit gives you all of it.

## How the bus test works

The name comes from an old, grim phrase. If the person who runs the business were hit by a bus, could the work go on? A car accident is the rare case. More often it is a hospital stay, a family emergency, or two weeks away with no phone. The test is the same for all of them.

You score five functions. For each one you answer three yes-or-no questions in order and stop at the first no.

1. Is anything written down?
2. Could a capable stranger follow it for two weeks without calling you?
3. Has someone other than you actually done it from the page?

A first no scores zero. All three yeses score three. Five functions at three points each is a total out of 15. The functions are money and billing, operations and fulfillment, customers and relationships, suppliers and tools, and people and knowledge. The [questionnaire](free/bus-test-questionnaire.md) spells it all out.

## What this does not do

It does not touch your accounts, collect your logins or give legal advice. A runbook records where a login is kept, never the login. The score is a map. It is not a grade.
