# What David Built for the Continuity Kit

This page is the story behind the kit, written for anyone deciding whether David Luther can do this kind of work. It covers the problem he saw, what he made, and the skills it took.

## The problem

Most small organizations run on one person. A salon owner knows how the supplier orders work, a pantry coordinator holds the church contacts, and a treasurer is the only one who can log in to the bank. Everyone knows this is risky, and the plan to write it all down gets postponed because it feels huge and nobody can say where to start. The usual outside answer is a consultant, an interview and a binder. The binder is often written for a generic company and sits unopened.

## What he made

David Luther built the [bus test](glossary.md#bus-test) while working with small business owners at Pigasus Group. It turns a vague worry into a question with an answer. Could the work go on if one person disappeared tomorrow? Five functions of any business are scored from zero to three, using three yes-or-no questions asked in order, and the scores add up to one total that falls in a band an owner can understand at a glance.

He kept the design deliberately simple. Every business answers the same questions, so a salon and a food pantry get scored by the same ruler. A weakest function gets named, though the score is a map and not a grade, and each function carries a target because not every function needs a complete handoff. Risk sets the order, with whatever would stop the money or the work soonest coming first.

Three [runbook](glossary.md#runbook) templates cover the places that fail most often, which are money, daily operations and access. The access template is built around [recovery paths](glossary.md#recovery-path), because the password is rarely the hard part. A runbook records where logins are kept, never the logins. Each document ends in a [two-week test](glossary.md#two-week-test), where someone other than the owner does a real task from the page while the owner stays out of it.

For this public kit he rebuilt the work as a Google Sheet with an [Apps Script](glossary.md#apps-script) [check-up](glossary.md#check-up) that tests structure without reading any of the owner's sentences, plus instructions for an [AI Project](glossary.md#ai-project) that interviews the owner one question at a time and refuses to guess. A full version is deliberately not planned, since no software can write down what is in someone's head.

## What it changed

Owners get a single number to start from, so the first conversation is about the gap and not about the size of the problem. The two-week test catches what a binder never does, which is the step the owner forgot to mention because they do it without thinking. Scoring again afterward shows whether the work landed.

## Skills it shows

- Turning a fuzzy risk into a repeatable measure that a non-specialist can use.
- Process documentation that captures judgment as well as steps.
- Designing for privacy. No passwords in documents, and a check-up that counts without reading.
- Writing [AI model](glossary.md#ai-model) instructions that interview, refuse to guess and stop at the first no.
- Technical writing for readers with no background, with a glossary, step IDs and a fixes table.
