# Glossary

Plain meanings for every technical term in this kit. Entries are in alphabetical order.

**AI model.** A computer program trained on huge amounts of writing that answers questions and drafts text. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are products built on AI models.

**AI Project.** A saved workspace inside ChatGPT or Claude where you keep standing instructions and reference files, so you do not repeat them in every chat. Think of it as a desk that stays set up between visits.

**Apps Script.** Google's built-in way to add small programs to a Google Sheet. It is how the check-up button works. It runs inside your own Google account.

**Bus test.** A thought experiment. If the person who runs the business disappeared tomorrow, could a capable stranger keep the work going for two weeks from what is written down? The name comes from the old phrase "hit by a bus".

**Check-up.** A button in the Sheet (Kit help menu, then Run check-up) that tests whether your setup is right and prints a short support code. It counts tabs and answers. It never reads the sentences you wrote.

**CSV.** A plain text file where each row of a spreadsheet is one line and the columns are separated by commas. Almost every program can export one, and every spreadsheet can open one.

**Custom GPT.** A version of ChatGPT you set up with your own instructions and files. It is one of the ways to use this kit's instructions.

**Domain registrar.** The company where your web address (your domain name) is bought and renewed. Losing access to it can take your website and your email address offline.

**Google Sheets.** Google's free spreadsheet app. You open it in a web browser at sheets.google.com.

**Knowledge file.** A document you upload to a Custom GPT or an AI Project so the AI model can read it every time.

**Password manager.** A program that stores your logins behind one strong password and can share them safely with a second person. Many have a free tier.

**Recovery path.** The steps for getting back into an account when the normal login does not work, such as backup codes, a recovery email and a phone number.

**Runbook.** A written guide to how one part of the work runs, detailed enough that a stranger could follow it. This kit has three, for money, operations and access.

**Support code.** A short code the check-up prints, such as CK-FR-01-5-E04-v0.1.0-7KQ. It tells whoever is helping which step, which problem and which kit version.

**Tab.** One of the labeled pages along the bottom of a spreadsheet. A single Google Sheets file can hold many.

**Two-factor method.** A second check after your password, such as a text message code or an app on your phone. Also called two-step sign-in.

**Two-week test.** A stand-in does one real task from a runbook while you stay out of it. The places they get stuck show what is still missing.

**What-if.** A result worked out with at least one number you assumed instead of measured. It shows how the math works and is never a finding about your business.
