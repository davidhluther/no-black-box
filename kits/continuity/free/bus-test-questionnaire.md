# The Bus Test Questionnaire

Print this page or copy it into a Google Doc. It has the five functions every business has, three yes-or-no questions for each, and a place to write what would break first. The point is to find the gap while nothing is on fire.

The [bus test](../glossary.md#bus-test) asks one thing. If the person who runs this business disappeared tomorrow, could a capable stranger keep the work going for two weeks from what is written down? Disappeared can mean a car accident, a hospital stay, a long flight or a sudden move. The name comes from the old phrase "hit by a bus". Nobody expects it. Everybody has a week where it would help to be ready.

## How scoring works

Each function gets one score from zero to three. Answer the three questions in order and stop at the first no.

| Score | You stopped at | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Question 1 is no | Nothing is written down. It lives in one head |
| 1 | Question 2 is no | Some notes exist, but a stranger would need to call you |
| 2 | Question 3 is no | A stranger could mostly follow it, but nobody has tried |
| 3 | All three are yes | Someone other than you has run it from the page |

Five functions at three points each makes a total out of 15. Higher totals mean the work could go on without you.

| Total | Band |
|---|---|
| 13 to 15 | Runnable by a stranger |
| 10 to 12 | Mostly runnable |
| 5 to 9 | Runnable in parts |
| 0 to 4 | Runs on one person |

Not every function needs to reach a 3. Some work you will always do yourself, and for those the goal is a stranger who can cover for two weeks, not forever. Decide your target score for each function before you start.

## The questions

For every function the three questions are the same.

1. Is anything about how this works written down anywhere?
2. Could a capable stranger follow what is written for two weeks without calling you?
3. Has someone other than you actually done it from the page?

### Money and billing

How invoices go out, how you get paid, what bills are due and when, payroll, taxes, and where the bank accounts are. Ask yourself what happens to the money if you cannot log in for two weeks.

### Operations and fulfillment

How a normal day runs, from opening to closing, and how the core work gets done, whether that is a haircut, a meal, a repair or a case. Ask yourself whether a stranger could open the doors and do one full cycle of the work.

### Customers and relationships

Who your regular customers or clients are, how they book or reorder, who handles complaints, and the promises you have made that nobody else knows about. Ask yourself who answers the phone and what they would say.

### Suppliers and tools

Who supplies what, on what schedule, and the systems the work runs on, such as the register, the booking tool and the shared drive. Ask yourself who to call when the main tool is down and how to get back into it.

### People and knowledge

Who does what, who covers whom, what each person knows that nobody else does, and how a new person gets trained. Ask yourself what the business loses every time someone leaves.

## For each function, write two sentences

- If you vanished. What would stop first, and how many days until it hurt?
- Written down. Where does anything written live, and when was it last right?

## Target scores

Write the score you want for each function after this exercise. A function that would stop the money within days is a first priority, even if you like it as it is.
