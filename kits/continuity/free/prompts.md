# Paste-In Prompts for the Continuity Kit

These work in any free [AI model](../glossary.md#ai-model), including free ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. Copy a prompt, replace the bracketed parts, and paste it into a new chat. Never paste a password, a recovery code, an account number or a card number. An AI model only needs to know where something is kept, not what it is.

## 1. Run the bus test with me

```
Run the "bus test" on my business. The question is whether the work could go on
if the person who runs it disappeared tomorrow, with a capable stranger keeping
it going for two weeks from what is written down.

There are five functions: Money and billing, Operations and fulfillment,
Customers and relationships, Suppliers and tools, People and knowledge.
For each one, ask me three questions in order and stop at the first no:
1. Is anything about how this works written down anywhere?
2. Could a capable stranger follow it for two weeks without calling me?
3. Has someone other than me actually done it from the page?
A first no scores 0, a second no scores 1, a third no scores 2, all yes scores 3.
If I am unsure, count it as no. Ask one function at a time and wait for my answer.
Afterward give me the total out of 15, the band (13 to 15 runnable by a stranger,
10 to 12 mostly runnable, 5 to 9 runnable in parts, 0 to 4 runs on one person),
my weakest and strongest function, and what to write first. Do not ask for
passwords or account numbers. My business is [one sentence about what you do].
```

## 2. Interview me for the money runbook

```
Interview me to fill in a money runbook for my business, one question at a time.
Use these sections in order: Where the money lives, The monthly cycle, Getting paid,
Paying out, The numbers to check, Who to call. Write my answers in my own words.
Where I do not know, leave a blank "______" and do not guess. Never ask for a
password or full account number. Say "last four digits" if you need an account.
At the end, show the finished runbook and list every blank.
```

## 3. Interview me for the operations runbook

```
Interview me to fill in an operations runbook, one question at a time. Sections in
order: Opening, The core cycle, Closing, When something breaks, Suppliers and tools
you lean on. Ask for the judgment as well as the steps, for example "what do you
check that a new person would miss?" Write in my own words, leave "______" for
anything I do not know, and do not invent steps. Show the result and list the blanks.
```

## 4. Interview me for the access runbook

```
Interview me to fill in an access runbook. I will never give you a password.
Ask only where things are kept and how to get back in. Sections in order: which
password manager the business uses and who has emergency access, then for each
critical account who owns it, which email and phone are tied to it, where the
recovery codes are kept, which second-check method is used, and the steps to
recover it. Then ask about the one email address everything resets through and
what to do if I am locked out. If I start to paste a secret, stop me.
```

## 5. Test my runbook

```
Here is a section of my runbook with any secrets removed. Pretend you are a capable
stranger covering for me for two weeks. List every place you would have to call me
because something is missing, unclear or assumed. Do not fix it yet. Just list the gaps
in order of how soon they would hurt.
[paste one section]
```

## 6. Score again after the two-week test

```
Last time I scored [total] out of 15 with these function scores: [list].
Since then, [name or role] ran [task] from the runbook while I stayed out of it.
They got stuck at [where]. Re-ask me the three questions for any function that
changed, and give me the new total and band. If the score did not move, say so.
```
