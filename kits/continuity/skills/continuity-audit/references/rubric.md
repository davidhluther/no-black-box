# Bus Test Rubric

The bus test asks one thing. If the person who runs this business disappeared tomorrow, could a capable stranger keep the work going for two weeks from what is written down?

Five functions are scored. All five are always scored, because skipping one only hides the worst score.

1. Money and billing. Invoicing, getting paid, bills, payroll, taxes, bank accounts.
2. Operations and fulfillment. A normal day from opening to closing, and the core cycle of the work.
3. Customers and relationships. Who the customers are, how they book or reorder, complaints, promises made.
4. Suppliers and tools. Who supplies what, the systems the work runs on, who to call when one is down.
5. People and knowledge. Who does what, who covers whom, what only one person knows, how a new person learns.

## Scoring each function

Ask three questions in order and stop at the first no.

1. Is anything about how this works written down anywhere?
2. Could a capable stranger follow what is written for two weeks without calling the owner?
3. Has someone other than the owner actually done it from the page?

| Score | Stopped at | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Q1 is no | Nothing written. It lives in one head |
| 1 | Q2 is no | Some notes, but a stranger would need to call |
| 2 | Q3 is no | A stranger could mostly follow it, never tried |
| 3 | all yes | Someone else has run it from the page |

Total out of 15. Bands: 13 to 15 runnable by a stranger, 10 to 12 mostly runnable, 5 to 9 runnable in parts, 0 to 4 runs on one person.

## Ties and what to do first

- The weakest function is the lowest score. A tie goes to the more fundamental one in the order above, money first.
- Sequence by risk, not tidiness. A function scoring 0 or 1 that would stop the money or the work within days outranks a 2 that would only be inconvenient.
- Pick two or three to start. A project that tries to document everything at once documents nothing.
- Not every function needs a 3. Agree a target score per function with the owner before writing anything.

## Rules for the scorer

- Score only what the owner tells you or shows you. A guess about a business is not a score.
- If an answer is unsure, treat it as no.
- "It's in my head" counts as not written down.
- A document nobody has opened in a year counts as written but probably wrong. Note that.
- Never ask for, accept or repeat a password, a recovery code or an account number.

## The people function and the cost of churn

When a role lives only in one head, the business pays to re-teach it every time someone leaves. Cost per departure is onboarding hours times the loaded hourly wage. Cost per year is departures times that. If the owner did not give the three inputs, show the arithmetic with example values and label it a what-if. Never present it as a finding.

## The two-week test

Have someone who is not the owner do one task from the document alone while the owner stays out of it. Where they get stuck is the next blank to fill. Then score again. If the score did not move, the work did not land.
